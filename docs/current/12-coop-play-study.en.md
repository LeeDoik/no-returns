Following the [stream-oriented idea exploration](11-streamer-concepts.en.md), the user requested representative cooperative games to play and study controls, UI and systems. These selections serve analysis rather than sales rankings. Genres, supported player counts and core features were checked against official Steam descriptions. Detailed control feel and UI quality are questions for hands-on observation, not completed evaluations. All have Steam purchase pages; Korean-account prices were not verified.

## Recommended games

| Game and official source | Maximum players: PC online, without mods | Rationale and observation tasks |
|---|---:|---|
| [PEAK](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3527290/PEAK/) | 4 | Cooperative climbing. Observe predictability of grabbing, hanging and falling; stamina and status presentation; rescuing friends and sharing tools. | [Lethal Company](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1966720/Lethal_Company/) | 4 | Horror extraction with a profit quota. Observe movement and carrying constraints; information differences among scans, radar and terminals; field versus ship support roles; return decisions and post-death waiting. Early Access. |
| [Human Fall Flat](https://store.steampowered.com/app/477160/Human_Fall_Flat/) | 8 | Physics puzzle platforming. Start with PEAK → R.E.P.O. → Overcooked! 2 to compare movement and rescue, object handling, and information delivery and division of labor. Add Deep Rock Galactic first if the new direction emphasizes combat. Add Human Fall Flat for physical controls or Lethal Company for horror and asymmetric information. This order is design judgment, not a popularity or enjoyment guarantee.

## Hands-on comparison method

Initially use the same group of 2–4 friends and the same input devices. Record player count, language, version, key bindings, voice mode and host status. Use the base game as the baseline and disclose any mods. Start with in-game voice where available. Suggested schedule: learn without guides for the first 20 minutes, then repeat actions and observe for 40–60 minutes. These are study time proposals, not completion times or sufficient mastery estimates.

1. Controls: do repeated grabs, releases and jumps behave as expected? Does failure feel related to input, camera, collision or networking? Separate impressions from confirmed causes.
2. UI: where did you learn the next objective, your danger and teammate status? Capture missed instructions and record time spent finding information.
3. Cooperation: separate moments requiring a teammate from those unchanged by solo action. Watch whether the most experienced player merely directs everyone else.
4. Randomness: distinguish map, item and enemy variation, authored events, and player mistakes or physical interactions. Check whether repeating the same conditions allows different choices.
5. Streaming: record brief footage around funny incidents. Without audio, can a viewer understand who caused what? How long does a dead player wait before acting again?

Record format: time / objective / input or choice / expectation / actual outcome / visual and audio feedback / emotion / causal hypothesis / reproduction status / transferable design principle.

For each game, record 3 strengths, 3 frustrations and 1 principle to adopt. Official descriptions were checked; installation, connectivity, controls, UI, fun and streaming were not tested hands-on. No decision changes the existing game's physical controls or approved direction.

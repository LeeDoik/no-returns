# NO RETURNS world-setting proposal

[한국어](world-setting.ko.md)

2026-09-17 · WORLD-01 · Narrative proposal; no gameplay implementation changes

## Status and direction

User-supplied premises: Earth's destruction; humanity scattered across planets; creatures beyond familiar expectations; underground habitation; dependence on deliveries of necessities; player couriers; suppression devices that cannot operate for long. The history, society, device explanation, clues and copy below are **unapproved proposals** expanding those premises. Earth's cause of destruction, creature origins, the company's ultimate purpose and the ending remain undecided.

Do not reuse the reference games' specific organizations, events or iconography. This interpretation uses optimistic corporate messaging against harsh reality, confined underground communities and everyday negotiations for survival. Retain the existing PSX space mystery and cooperative accidents during serious work.

Three possible centers were considered. The recommendation is a **corporate-maintained survival logistics network**: it directly supports deliveries and progression and can be conveyed with modest environmental production. Political conflict between underground cities would enrich society but expand dialogue and faction content. Foregrounding cosmic phenomena would be striking but could weaken the human reason for delivering necessities. Center the recommendation and use the other approaches as background and occasional clues.

## 1. After Earth disappeared

When Earth broke apart, humanity already had mines, research stations and temporary settlements on other planets. The survivors did not arrive in new worlds to begin civilization again. They were stranded in facilities whose supplies from Earth had stopped.

Interstellar freighters and some factories survived. No settlement, however, can manufacture everything it needs. One produces nutrient cultures, another air filters, another medicine and machine parts. Mines must keep running to buy medicine; medicine must arrive for miners to work their next shift.

Accounts of Earth's final day disagree: textbooks describe an accident, monuments describe a war, and shipping records show vessels withdrawing a day before the event. Destruction is factual within the setting; its cause remains a mystery. Do not assume the creatures originated because Earth was destroyed.

## 2. People moved underground

Surface creatures do not behave as humans expect. Some navigate by sound; others stand still like structures during daylight; nobody has seen the complete body of certain enormous organisms. Each has observable rules, but humanity has not understood their whole ecology.

Early surface bases gradually moved into mines and underground maintenance facilities. Thick rock, narrow winding approaches and successive acoustic and isolation doors protect residential areas. Underground does not mean safe from every species. Abandoned shafts and outer walls remain dangerous, and surface delivery buildings form a buffer separate from habitation. This avoids requiring continuous suppression just to keep settlements alive.

Cities develop different lives. Old mining towns sleep to shift sirens; former research stations distribute food using hospital passes. Parking structures become markets, and photographs of Earth landscapes take the place of windows and raise housing prices. Children have never seen a blue sky, but schools still teach them to color it blue.

Not every resident is hopeless or cruel. Shared meals, arguments, celebrations and small kindnesses must survive if deliveries are to matter.

## 3. Delivery companies outlasted governments

Companies owning freighters, route maps and supply depots connect the cities. Residents may hate a company, but know where their next filters come from. Company employees also know that a broken logistics network kills people. Avoid making the entire organization pointlessly evil.

Yet company city lists are updated by delivery records rather than people. Addresses without receipt confirmations for a long period become inactive and lose their routes. Residents can remain alive while their city disappears from the service map.

For residents, a delivery means survival; for the company, it means a contract and maintenance cost. A surface advertisement still reads "YOUR WORLD. STILL CONNECTED." Immediately below it, small print lists excluded service regions.

This social premise does not automatically add permanent player debt, confiscated wages or save loss. Economy and failure-rule changes require separate design and validation.

## 4. Why we became couriers

Employees are contract transport workers from underground cities. One may want better housing for family; another may want to leave a cramped city. Leave room for players to imagine individual backgrounds. Do not add free spacecraft piloting.

Company equipment is repeatedly patched, and markings left by generations of crews overlap inside the ship. Next to the official safety instructions, somebody has written, "If the radio calls your name, do not answer." This is an ambiguous narrative prop—a superstition or an experience—not a newly confirmed attack mechanic.

Pay buys better equipment and access to harder routes. As crews progress, the surface does not simply become safe; the company sends them to stranger places. Workers need not be chosen saviors of the universe to keep a city's air supply running until tomorrow.

## 5. Suppression creates a brief working window

The proposed mechanism is a high-output resonance field. Some local large creatures experience it as an intolerable stimulus and avoid approaching. It is not a physical barrier or invulnerable zone, so Listeners already established inside buildings and pipes may remain.

Sustained operation degrades the resonator's core elements until they can no longer maintain output. Cooling and stabilization are necessary: swapping a battery or briefly switching the device off cannot indefinitely extend safety during the same delivery. Its scale requires fixed infrastructure, distinguishing it from portable lure beacons. This document sets no exact restart duration or equipment price.

As a delivery ship approaches, local infrastructure temporarily secures the work area. Warning cadence, tower lights, radio interference and distant creature behavior convey the remaining margin. Crews still have an opportunity to retreat as the system weakens; outer creatures enter after shutdown. Indirect timing follows the existing direction. Narrative mystery must not turn gameplay warnings into lies.

## 6. Why land and carry cargo on foot?

Delivery depots are not exposed entrances directly above living quarters. They are surface inspection, quarantine and unloading facilities. Transfer machinery and isolation doors connect them to underground residential areas. Couriers deliver to the receiving station without entering residents' homes.

Maintained landing sites are restricted to places with adequate flat ground, load capacity and approach space. Old roofs and equipment around buildings are not safe alternative landing pads. Do not arbitrarily prohibit every drone or communication system. Automated transport can operate along maintained sections, while humans handle the last stretch of abandoned approaches, obstacles and changed creature routes. Dropping precision equipment and medicine from above cannot replace careful delivery.

Paper receipts can serve as local proof when communications are delayed. Preserve the current reward-on-receipt-retrieval rule; do not add a new requirement to submit receipts aboard the ship.

## 7. The story of CINDER DEPOT

This was originally a surface freight depot supporting settlement construction. Surface employees withdrew, but receiving equipment serving the underground settlement still operates. Meeting no people outdoors does not establish that every resident has disappeared.

The separate Unity blockout implements space and markers only. These clues, functioning vertical transfer machinery, facility interactions and residents are **unimplemented proposals**. This narrative document does not mark them implemented.

## 8. Mystery gradually departs from ordinary deliveries

Most deliveries should contain goods that people actually need. If every parcel is suspicious from the first shift, players lose a baseline for distinguishing ordinary from strange.

The first layer is inconsistent company records: recent deliveries to a closed address or disagreements between registers and receipts. The second is physical evidence: fresh wheel tracks in a dusty closed facility or the same handwriting on another planet. Only at the third layer does a sender code associated with destroyed Earth appear.

Possible explanations include outdated automated orders, residents concealed by the company or something imitating human procedures. All remain hypotheses, not answers. Earth's destruction, alien ecology and corporate secrets need not share a single cause.

The central question evolves from "What are these creatures?" to "Are we still delivering to places where people live?" Mandatory deliveries must remain completable without discovering clues.

## 9. Proposed opening delivery sequence

A cheerful voice in new-employee training says, "Civilization is on its way." A green Earth briefly appears. The film was produced before Earth disappeared.

The first CINDER DEPOT shipment contains air filters marked for priority supply to an underground hospital ward. When employees reach BAY 04, a speaker announces acceptance and a receipt prints. A moment later, transfer machinery moves below. Without showing residents, the sound suggests that the shipment has reached somewhere that needed it.

On the way back, players may find an office evacuation notice: "Civilian evacuation of upper facilities complete." It does not prove that the underground settlement closed. Yet the contract terminal lists the entire address as inactive. It could be an error. Another delivery will still be assigned.

Back aboard the ship, the settlement screen shows the pay already secured. The bottom of the report reads, "Proof of receipt does not verify the recipient."

## 10. Title and proposed game copy

NO RETURNS can refer to an Earth nobody can return to, a regulation against taking goods back out of quarantine intake, and a contract that does not guarantee the crew's return. It does not prohibit teammate rescue or actual extraction.
